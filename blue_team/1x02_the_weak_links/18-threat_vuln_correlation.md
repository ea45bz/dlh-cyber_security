### Which single vulnerability would cause the most damage?

**Finding 003 (PostgreSQL unrestricted network access)** is the single finding that, if exploited, can yield the greatest overall harm given the full threat context:

1. **Asset Criticality** – `ehr‑db-01` contains PHI and billing data; loss or exposure triggers HIPAA violations and financial liability.
2. **Attack Surface** – The database accepts connections from every host in the flat 10.10.0.0/16 subnet, so _any_ compromised workstation (whether from a ransomware drop‑in or an attacker who first exploited Billing Server’s RCE) can immediately read/write all patient records without needing further credentials.
3. **Lateral Mobility** – Once PHI is in the attacker’s hands, credential theft or privilege escalation on other systems becomes easier; attackers can then pivot to EHR, DC, MRI, and medical devices.
4. **Regulatory Impact** – Unauthorized disclosure of PHI triggers 100 k+ penalty per HIPAA breach, legal exposure, and reputational damage.

While the combination of Findings 001/002 (billing RCE + privilege escalation) is a potent vector, it still requires an initial foothold on the billing server before accessing the database. The PostgreSQL misconfiguration bypasses that prerequisite entirely, making it the most dangerous single point. Consequently, patching or segmenting `ehr‑db-01` should be treated as the highest priority remediation action.
