### Asset Criticality Matrix

| **Asset Category**              | **Confidentiality** | **Integrity** | **Availability** | **Overall Criticality** | **Justification**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------- | ------------- | ---------------- | ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **EHR System**                  | Critical            | Critical      | Critical         | Critical                | EHR‑SRV‑01 hosts the _patient‑care_ application that delivers clinical orders, results and documentation. The associated database (EHR‑DB‑01) stores PHI for all ~50 k active patients, billing codes, medication histories and treatment plans. A breach or loss of integrity here directly jeopardises patient safety, triggers mandatory HIPAA reporting, and can lead to multi‑million‑dollar litigation and regulatory sanctions. |
| **Imaging / PACS**              | Critical            | Critical      | Critical         | Critical                | PACS‑SRV‑01 is the sole repository for all diagnostic imaging (X‑ray, CT, MRI). Loss of image integrity leads to incorrect diagnoses; unavailability stalls treatment decisions and can delay critical interventions. The system contains protected health information that, if exfiltrated, could be monetised or used in ransomware extortion.                                                                                       |
| **Billing Infrastructure**      | Critical            | Critical      | Critical         | Critical                | BILLING‑SRV‑01 and associated services manage financial transactions for every patient visit. Compromise here risks massive revenue loss, insurance fraud, and potential audit exposure. Availability is critical because billing cycles are time‑sensitive; any downtime directly impacts cash flow.                                                                                                                                  |
| **Network Core & VPN**          | Critical            | Critical      | Critical         | Critical                | The combination of AP‑1F‑01/ER, NETGEAR‑ROUTER, and the FortiGate gateway forms the backbone that interconnects all subnets. These devices enforce authentication (AD‑DC‑01) and network segmentation. A breach or outage would give an attacker lateral movement across the entire hospital network, disrupting every other asset and exposing PHI at scale.                                                                          |
| **Medical IoT Devices**         | High                | Critical      | High             | Critical                | Philips IntelliVue monitors (MON‑ICU‑01) and BD Alaris pumps (PUMP‑ICU‑01) provide real‑time vital‑sign monitoring and drug delivery. Tampering or denial of service can directly cause patient harm. These devices also expose web interfaces to the flat network; any compromise could allow credential theft from other systems or remote control of therapy.                                                                       |
| **Clinical Workstations**       | High                | Medium        | High             | High                    | WS‑RECEPT‑01, WS‑NURSE‑1F‑01, etc., are the primary user interfaces for clinicians accessing the EHR, order entry and medication administration. While they do not store PHI locally, compromise leads to credential theft and lateral movement. Availability is crucial to maintain workflow during patient care.                                                                                                                     |
| **Administrative Workstations** | High                | Medium        | High             | High                    | WS‑ADMIN‑01, WS‑PHARM‑01, WS‑LAB‑01, etc., host administrative tools, payroll systems and billing interfaces. Loss of integrity can lead to data manipulation, while availability is essential for day‑to‑day operations.                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Physical Security Systems**   | Low                 | Critical      | High             | High                    | BADGE‑READER‑MAIN, NURSE‑CALL‑01 and similar devices control building access. Compromise can allow unauthorised entry to clinical areas, potentially exposing PHI or enabling sabotage of critical systems.                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| **Backup & Storage**            | Critical            | Critical      | Critical         | Critical                | BACKUP‑SRV‑01 and NAS‑01 hold the entire data replication pipeline for EHR, imaging, billing and audit logs. Loss of backup integrity or availability would cripple disaster recovery and could result in irrecoverable loss of patient records, triggering regulatory penalties.                                                                                                                                                      |
| **Shadow IT / Unmanaged**       | Medium              | Low           | Low              | Medium                  | UNKNOWN‑01 (10.10.2.99) and UNKNOWN‑02 (10.10.10.200) are unmanaged Linux hosts with exposed web services. They present unknown risk vectors for exfiltration or pivoting, but because they are not documented or monitored, the immediate impact is currently limited compared to critical systems.                                                                                                                                   |

### Top 5 Most Critical Assets

| **Asset ID** | **Name**                                               | **Why It’s Critical **                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| ASSET014     | EHR‑SRV‑01 (Electronic Health Records server)          | The primary application that orchestrates all clinical workflows – order entry, results viewing, medication reconciliation and patient charts. With ~50 k patients under care, the server holds vast amounts of PHI, billing codes, genetic data, and compliance logs. Any breach, tampering or downtime would directly jeopardise patient safety (e.g., wrong medication orders), trigger HIPAA reporting requirements, and expose the hospital to multi‑million dollar fines. The EHR is also a prerequisite for most downstream systems; thus it is the single point that all other critical services depend upon. |
| ASSET015     | EHR‑DB‑01 (PostgreSQL database)                        | This relational database contains the back‑end of the EHR system, storing patient demographics, clinical notes, lab results and imaging references. Loss of integrity here would corrupt medical records; a data breach could expose millions of PHI entries at once. Availability is critical: any outage stalls clinician access to vital information during care delivery. The database also supports billing, analytics, and audit trails – its compromise could trigger simultaneous operational, financial, and regulatory crises.                                                                              |
| ASSET016     | PACS‑SRV‑01 (Picture Archiving & Communication System) | All diagnostic images flow through this server. If an attacker alters image data or denies access, diagnoses become unreliable; the risk of missed disease detection is real. The system also stores imaging PHI, and its downtime can stall treatments that rely on imaging (e.g., radiation oncology planning). The PACS’s integration with EHR means any compromise propagates PHI leakage across systems.                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ASSET018     | AD‑DC‑01 (Active Directory Domain Controller)          | AD‑DC‑01 is the sole authentication backbone for the entire network – from workstations to servers, printers and IoT devices. Compromise of this device would grant an attacker domain‑wide access, enabling credential theft, lateral movement, data exfiltration, or ransomware propagation. It also governs policy enforcement (e.g., MFA). Loss of integrity here could allow a rogue admin to create backdoor accounts without audit trails, rendering all security controls ineffective.                                                                                                                        |
| ASSET024     | MON‑ICU‑01 (Philips IntelliVue ICU monitor)            | This bedside monitor continuously tracks vitals and administers critical alarms for ICU patients. Firmware or web UI compromise can alter readings, suppress alarms, or inject false data – all of which can directly cause patient harm or even death. The device also integrates with the EHR to flag adverse events; tampering could erase these alerts from clinical records, undermining safety monitoring and violating regulatory standards (e.g., FDA 21 CFR Part 820). Its web interface is publicly exposed on the flat network, providing a low‑effort attack vector for malicious actors.                 |
